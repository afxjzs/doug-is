# Writing style: sound human, not AI

Prose that reads as machine-written shares a small set of tells. Cut them. This
list is adapted from Wikipedia's "Signs of AI writing"
(https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Signs_of_AI_writing). It applies to
blog posts, docs, PR text, and messages. Not code or technical terms.

Goal: shorter, plainer, more direct. When a sentence can be cut or a word
swapped for a common one without losing meaning, do it.

## Words and phrases to cut

- **Inflated vocabulary.** delve, crucial, pivotal, vital, robust, comprehensive,
  nuanced, multifaceted, meticulous, intricate, seamless, testament, tapestry,
  landscape, realm, underscore, showcase, foster, garner, leverage, boast,
  bolster, vibrant, rich, profound. Replace with a plain word or drop it.
- **Fake-importance framing.** "stands as," "serves as," "plays a crucial role,"
  "is a testament to," "underscores the importance of," "highlights the
  significance of," "leaves an indelible mark," "marks a shift," "reflects a
  broader." Say the plain fact instead.
- **Promotional verbs for "is."** serves as, stands as, functions as, represents,
  boasts, features. Usually just: is, has.
- **Vague attribution.** "industry experts," "observers note," "many argue,"
  "studies show," "it is widely regarded." Name the source or drop the claim.
- **Filler transitions.** "Additionally," "Moreover," "Furthermore,"
  "It's important to note that," "It's worth mentioning." Start with the point.

## Sentence shapes to avoid

- **Negative parallelism.** "It's not just X, it's Y." "Not only ... but also ..."
  "This isn't about X; it's about Y." State the thing directly.
- **Rule of three.** Decorative triples of adjectives or phrases ("fast, simple,
  and reliable") used for rhythm rather than meaning. Keep the words that carry
  information; drop the rest.
- **Trailing `-ing` summary clauses.** "..., highlighting its importance."
  "..., ensuring a seamless experience." "..., reflecting a broader trend." These
  tack on empty analysis. Cut them or make them a real, separate sentence.
- **Elegant variation.** Swapping in synonyms to avoid repeating a word. Repeat
  the word; it's clearer.
- **Avoiding plain "is."** "ventured into politics as a candidate" instead of
  "ran for office." Prefer the simple verb.
- **"Challenges and future" wrap-ups.** "Despite its challenges, X continues to
  thrive ..." Speculative, hollow closers. End on a concrete point.

## Punctuation and formatting

- **Em dashes.** Overused to splice clauses. Prefer a period, comma, or
  parentheses. (This repo's owner: no em dashes.)
- **Bold sprinkled everywhere.** Emphasis loses force when every other phrase is
  bold. Bold sparingly, if at all.
- **Heading case.** Title Case, matching every existing post on the site.
  Wikipedia's guide lists Title Case as an AI tell; here it's the house style and
  predates the bots. Consistency across the catalog wins.
- **Inline-header lists** ("• **Thing**: description") where flowing prose reads
  better. Use a list only for genuinely list-shaped content.

## Tone

- Prefer the active voice.
- Write like a person talking to another person, not a press release or a
  brochure. No "nestled in the heart of," no "in today's fast-paced world."
- Concrete beats grand. A specific detail beats a claim about significance.

## Before delivering any prose, check

1. Can any sentence be cut? Cut it.
2. Any long or fancy word with a plain equivalent? Swap it.
3. Any word from the cut-list above? Remove or replace it.
4. Any negative parallelism, decorative triple, or trailing `-ing` clause? Rewrite.
5. Any em dash? Replace it.
6. Read it aloud. If it doesn't sound like a person, rewrite it.
